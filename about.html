Why generic OCR fails on meters

Standard OCR engines (Tesseract, EasyOCR, default PaddleOCR) routinely fail on utility meters due to four specific failure modes:

Faceplate text clutter Generic text detectors latch onto printed serial numbers, barcodes, or manufacture dates rather than the active LCD register.
7-segment digit disconnection Models trained on continuous printed text break unlit LCD segment gaps into incorrect letters (0 to N, 8 to B).
Unit icon interference Adjacent unit markers (kWh, kW) merge into digits, causing 10x to 100x billing errors.
Leading-zero stripping Standard numeric parsers turn "007888.54" into "7888.54", corrupting billing reconciliation.

Pipeline Architecture

STAGE 1

YOLOv11n LCD detector

Locates and crops the active counter display from the full photo, discarding faceplate markings and serials.

STAGE 2

Dynamic crop normalization (96px height)

Crops are scaled to a standard 96px height with aspect ratio preserved. This resolution prevents decimal points from degrading below one pixel during resize.

STAGE 3

PP-OCRv6 SVTR-CTC recognizer

A CTC decoding head pruned strictly to 11 tokens (digits 0 to 9 and decimal point), making unit icons structurally impossible to output.

Dataset Splitting & Integrity

Early model iterations suffered from silent data leakage: photos of the same physical meter appeared in both training and validation sets. The dataset was rebuilt with strict physical meter grouping so images of any individual meter exist in only one split.
