Why generic OCR fails here

Tesseract, EasyOCR, and default PaddleOCR all collapse on utility meters, for four distinct, well-understood reasons — not just "the model wasn't fine-tuned."

Faceplate text clutter Text detectors latch onto printed serial numbers or manufacture years instead of the active LCD reading.
7-segment digit disconnection Models trained on continuous Latin strokes fracture unlit LCD segment gaps into wrong letters (0→N, 8→B).
Unit icon interference "kWh" / "kW" glyph borders get misread as extra trailing digits, risking 10–100× billing errors.
Leading-zero stripping Naive numeric coercion turns "007888.54" into "7888.54," silently corrupting billing reconciliation.

Three-stage architecture

STAGE 1

YOLOv11n LCD detector

Locates and crops the display out of the full meter photo, discarding faceplate clutter, barcodes, and serials. ~30ms on edge GPU.

STAGE 2

Dynamic crop normalization → fixed 96px height

The crop is resized to a canonical 96px height (width scales to preserve aspect ratio) and contrast-normalized. This exact resolution was chosen after an ablation traced decimal-point loss to a lower-resolution resize shrinking the physical decimal dot below one pixel.

STAGE 3

PP-OCRv6 SVTR-CTC recognizer

A CTC head pruned to exactly 11 tokens (0–9, "."), so unit-icon glyphs are structurally impossible to hallucinate as digits. ~45ms on edge GPU.

Dataset: fixing a leakage bug that hid a real failure

An early version of this pipeline looked strong until a splitting bug was found: photos of the same physical meter were appearing in both train and validation sets, masking a real collapse on industrial meters. The dataset was rebuilt with strict physical meter-grouped splitting — no meter's photos can appear in more than one split.

Roadmap

INT8 quantization to shrink the combined pipeline toward ~6–7MB for faster first-load; multi-threaded in-browser inference where hosting supports cross-origin isolation; progressive detection reuse in live video to cut per-frame cost further.
